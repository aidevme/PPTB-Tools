REUSABLE STORYBOARD PROMPT — HSO Knowledge intro video
=======================================================

Generates a six-panel black-and-white pencil storyboard for the
opening intro of any HSO Knowledge documentation app. The panels
visualize the same 10-second cinematic intro across products:

  1. Title card        (universal — never changes)
  2. Conference room   (universal — HSO Manhattan office)
  3. Manhattan view    (universal — 69th-floor Empire State)
  4. App-specific      ← FILL IN — story beat 1 about the app
  5. App-specific      ← FILL IN — story beat 2 about the app
  6. Brand end frame   (format universal — text fills in per app)

Panels 4 and 5 are a tiny two-beat story about THIS app. They might
be problem→solution, before→after, setup→payoff, many→one,
manual→automated, question→answer — whatever shape best captures
what the app does, who it serves, and why it matters. Don't force
an AI angle if AI isn't the headline.

Use this storyboard as the pre-production reference before generating
the 10-second video via Veo 3 / Gemini Omni. Once the look is locked
in the storyboard, the same six descriptions map directly to the
video prompt's five beats (panels 2+3 collapse into the dolly+tilt
beat for the video).


DISCOVERY-FIRST WORKFLOW (recommended)
=======================================

When an agent is invoked to generate a storyboard for an app, the
agent should DERIVE the placeholder values from the project itself
before asking the user. Most HSO Knowledge projects already contain
deep teaching-voice content that names the audience, the pain, and
the product's job-to-be-done in plain language. Read that first.

Files to consult, in priority order:

  1. *.narration.json — the per-deck hand-authored teaching narration.
     The executive deck (01-executive-overview) and sales deck
     (02-sales-enablement) are usually the most useful: they name
     the audience, the chaos the app solves, and the outcome.
  2. *-primer.md / *-primer.narration.json — industry or customer
     primer that frames the domain.
  3. README.md / SPEC-*.md / docs/*.md — written product context.
  4. The landing index.html section copy — short hero / "what this
     is" / "pick your role" blurbs are pre-distilled positioning.
  5. CLAUDE.md at project root, if present.

What to mine from those files:

  - APP_NAME and APP_TAGLINE: usually in the executive deck's cover
    slide or the README hero line.
  - Panel 4 artifact + caption: search the executive and sales decks
    for the chaos/pain that opens the story. The narration usually
    says it out loud ("brokers receive 200 PDF statements at
    month-end…").
  - Panel 5 outcome + caption: search the same decks for the payoff
    sentence ("…close in days instead of weeks," "…producers get
    paid on time," "…controllers see one ledger"). That's the "what
    this app makes possible" beat.

Only ask the user when discovery genuinely doesn't surface a
candidate. When you do ask, ask narrowly ("the exec deck mentions X
and Y as the pain — should panel 4 lean on X, Y, or something
else?") — never re-ask for things the project already states.

Once filled, hand the user the completed prompt with the four
reference image paths from the skill's references/ folder.

HOW TO PICK PANELS 4 AND 5 — THE TWO-BEAT STORY
================================================

Panels 1–3 introduce HSO. Panel 6 names the product. The middle two
panels are where THIS app gets to say what it's for. Treat them as a
tiny silent film: panel 4 is the "before" beat, panel 5 is the "what
this app makes possible" beat. They have to read as connected scenes,
not unrelated illustrations.

To pick well, answer three questions:

  1. WHO is this app for? Picture a real person — a controller, a
     plant manager, a benefits coordinator, a deal desk lead, a
     compliance officer. Either panel 4 or panel 5 should hint at
     that person's world.

  2. WHAT does the app actually do for them? Sum it up in one
     informal sentence ("it reconciles broker statements," "it gets
     payroll out on time," "it turns scattered contracts into one
     pipeline view"). That sentence is the bridge between panels 4
     and 5.

  3. WHAT'S THE TURN? Pick the story shape that makes the value
     unmistakable:

       - Problem → Solution: stacks of paper → clean unified record
       - Before → After: a workspace cluttered with sources → the
         same workspace with one dashboard glowing
       - Setup → Payoff: a person looking at a daunting task → the
         same person walking out the door with it handled
       - Many → One: dozens of disparate sources → a single
         consolidated view
       - Manual → Automated: a hand writing in a ledger → the
         ledger writing itself
       - Question → Answer: a question floating above the table → an
         answer with the source citation under it

If panels 4 and 5 could be swapped between two different apps without
changing meaning, you haven't picked well. Re-pick until a domain
expert who saw just those two panels would say "oh, that's an app
for ___" without needing panel 6 to tell them.


CATEGORY CHEAT SHEET
====================
Each row is a starting point — sharpen with the specific product's
terminology, audience, and outcome.

Financial / ERP / accounting:
  Panel 4 candidates : carrier statements, GL exports, vendor
                       invoices, bank rec reports, expense receipts
  Panel 5 candidates : a clean reconciled ledger; a CFO looking at
                       a single accurate dashboard; statements
                       resolving into one journal entry
  Story shape        : Problem → Solution; Many → One

CRM / sales / customer success:
  Panel 4 candidates : a deck of contract cards; a printed pipeline
                       in disarray; sticky-note renewal reminders
  Panel 5 candidates : a salesperson on a confident call; a tidy
                       pipeline view; renewal dates surfacing
                       weeks ahead of expiration
  Story shape        : Setup → Payoff; Question → Answer

HR / workforce / benefits:
  Panel 4 candidates : a pile of benefit enrollment forms;
                       handwritten timesheets; an overwhelmed HR
                       coordinator's desk
  Panel 5 candidates : an employee receiving their plan summary; a
                       payroll run completing on its own; a calm
                       HR coordinator at an empty desk
  Story shape        : Before → After; Manual → Automated

Procurement / supply chain:
  Panel 4 candidates : unmatched POs and packing slips on a desk;
                       a buyer holding two mismatched documents
  Panel 5 candidates : the three documents stacking into perfect
                       alignment; a buyer signing off and walking
                       away
  Story shape        : Many → One; Manual → Automated

Manufacturing / operations:
  Panel 4 candidates : a plant-floor clipboard of paper production
                       sheets; a shift supervisor holding a stack
                       of daily reports
  Panel 5 candidates : a production dashboard glowing on a screen;
                       the supervisor with hands free, looking at
                       the line; one screen replacing the binder
  Story shape        : Manual → Automated; Many → One

Field service / dispatch:
  Panel 4 candidates : a wall of paper service tickets pinned up;
                       a technician with a clipboard in a van
  Panel 5 candidates : a routed map with one optimized path; a
                       technician on-site finishing early
  Story shape        : Problem → Solution; Before → After

Compliance / audit / regulatory:
  Panel 4 candidates : a binder of evidence packets; a stressed
                       auditor reviewing controls one by one
  Panel 5 candidates : a clean attestation report; an auditor at a
                       conference table presenting confident findings
  Story shape        : Before → After; Setup → Payoff

Document AI / mailroom / intake:
  Panel 4 candidates : a mixed pile of incoming documents —
                       invoices, contracts, scanned letters — drawn
                       at varied angles
  Panel 5 candidates : the same documents sorted into three neat
                       output bins, each labeled by type
  Story shape        : Many → One; Manual → Automated


EXAMPLE FILL — ERP UNITE (insurance brokerage finance)
------------------------------------------------------
Story shape: Problem (chaos on the table) → Solution (clean unified
record).

  APP_NAME_UPPERCASE    = ERP UNITE
  APP_NAME_TITLECASE    = ERP Unite
  APP_TAGLINE           = Insurance brokerage finance, productized.

  APP_PANEL_4_HEADING   = TABLE WITH STATEMENTS
  APP_PANEL_4_VISUAL    = Top-down sketch of the conference table.
                          Resting on it: a fanned stack of insurance
                          carrier statements, edges curled, dense
                          tabular content suggested by faint rule
                          lines and tiny numbers, a coffee cup
                          beside them.
  APP_PANEL_4_CAPTION   = On the table waits the chaos every broker knows — two hundred carrier statements at month-end.

  APP_PANEL_5_HEADING   = ONE CLEAN LEDGER
  APP_PANEL_5_VISUAL    = Same table, but the stacks of statements
                          are gone. In their place: a single open
                          ledger or laptop screen showing neatly
                          tabulated rows that match the statements
                          to journal entries, with checkmarks down
                          the margin. A controller stands behind the
                          table, satisfied.
  APP_PANEL_5_CAPTION   = By month-end close, the same controller signs off on one clean ledger instead of two hundred PDFs.


EXAMPLE FILL — Manufacturing operations app
--------------------------------------------
Story shape: Manual → Automated.

  APP_PANEL_4_HEADING = THE SHIFT BEGINS
  APP_PANEL_4_VISUAL  = A plant-floor supervisor stands at a workbench
                        flipping through a thick clipboard of paper
                        production sheets, machines visible in the
                        background.
  APP_PANEL_4_CAPTION = Every shift starts with the supervisor flipping through paper to figure out what to run.

  APP_PANEL_5_HEADING = ONE SCREEN, FULL VIEW
  APP_PANEL_5_VISUAL  = The same workbench, but the clipboard is set
                        aside; a tablet on the bench shows a live
                        production dashboard with each machine's
                        status. The supervisor's hands are free.
  APP_PANEL_5_CAPTION = Now the same supervisor walks the floor with one screen telling them everything.


EXAMPLE FILL — Procurement / 3-way match app
---------------------------------------------
Story shape: Many → One.

  APP_PANEL_4_HEADING = THREE PILES, NO MATCH
  APP_PANEL_4_VISUAL  = A buyer's desk with three separate
                        document stacks labeled "POs," "Packing
                        Slips," and "Invoices" — none of them lining
                        up, the buyer's hand hovering over them.
  APP_PANEL_4_CAPTION = A buyer's morning starts with three piles of paper that don't agree.

  APP_PANEL_5_HEADING = ONE MATCHED RECORD
  APP_PANEL_5_VISUAL  = The same desk, but the three stacks have
                        collapsed into a single neat report with each
                        PO, slip, and invoice paired and checked off.
                        The buyer is already moving to the next task.
  APP_PANEL_5_CAPTION = By coffee break, every line is matched and the buyer is on to the next thing.
